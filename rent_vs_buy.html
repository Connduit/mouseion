Checklist of easy-to-forget costs

Already in the model

  • Mortgage interest, with the standard-vs-itemized deduction check
  • Property tax and the SALT deduction cap
  • Homeowners insurance, maintenance, HOA, PMI
  • Closing costs, move-in costs, and selling costs
  • Capital gains tax on the home, minus the sale exclusion
  • Yearly rent increases, as a percent or a dollar amount, and renters insurance
  • Selling the house at your horizon: agent fees, mortgage payoff, and capital gains tax
  • Renting out a room: income, empty months, extra costs, and tax on the profit
  • Renting out the whole house while you rent elsewhere, with depreciation, recapture, and an investment-loan rate bump
  • Keeping the house forever instead of selling it
  • Opportunity cost: the down payment and monthly savings invested instead
  • Tax on investment gains

Not modeled, so check these yourself

  • Special assessments or big HOA fee jumps
  • Property tax reassessment after you buy (your bill may start higher than the seller's)
  • Utilities and upkeep for a bigger space than you rent now
  • Furniture, appliances, and yard equipment
  • Mortgage points, and refinancing if rates drop
  • Flood, earthquake, or wind insurance in some areas
  • State-level mortgage interest or property tax breaks
  • Alternative minimum tax and income phase-outs on deductions
  • Sales tax and other cost-of-living differences between towns or states
  • Room-rental tax details: depreciation, recapture when you sell, and effects on the sale exclusion
  • Landlord risks: bad tenants, long vacancies, evictions, local rental rules and licensing
  • Investor loan rules: lenders often want 15–25% down on a rental, and may not let you rent out an owner-occupant loan right away
  • Rental-loss limits: unused losses carry forward here, but your own tax situation may differ
  • Rent control, or a landlord who might not renew
  • Job stability: selling within 2–3 years usually favors renting
  • Rental income if you'd house-hack or rent out a room
  • The non-financial side: stability, freedom to renovate, flexibility to move

This is a planning estimate, not tax or financial advice. Tax figures are simplified: one federal marginal rate, no bracket-crossing, no phase-outs, and no state deduction for mortgage interest. Standard deduction and SALT cap defaults are editable, so check current IRS figures. A tax professional or lender can firm up the real numbers.
